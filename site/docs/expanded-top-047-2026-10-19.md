## What qualifies

Discovery compares all six official interest scores against the full occupational profile before any profession-specific reflection. New candidates require a complete, distinct profile with a correlation of at least0.608, the existing editorial strong-interest band. This is pattern similarity, not a probability of success or a measure of aptitude.

After a neutral work preview, the student reflects on three distinct constructs: subject interest, a way of working and the role’s distinctive responsibility. All three need a supported Yes. A simulated biography inference needs medium or high confidence and specific evidence for each answer. Low confidence, uncertainty, unfamiliarity and a No cannot establish a Top match. One or two supported interests can produce a Needs confirmation offer with the exact remaining questions.

The prompts ask what the student would like to learn. Existing professional tools, specialist knowledge, prior work access and current competence are not prerequisites for interest. Actual admission, licensing, training and work experience remain separate feasibility questions. A role typically requiring experience is labeled as a destination.

When newly confirmed professions join the Top pool, ranking first compares the weakest defining-interest evidence confidence, then complete-profile alignment, with education preference only as a tie-break. It does not reward a shorter three-question check over an eight-question guided check. Salary and degree prestige cannot create eligibility or improve this common ranking. No degree quota or minimum number of matches is imposed. Existing ranking is preserved when no newly confirmed profession joins the pool.

## Financial and validation limits

Newly activated professions have no verified starting salary, education-cost scenario or lifestyle verdict in this release. Those values remain unknown. They never inherit another occupation’s pay and cannot appear as sustaining lifestyle merely because they become Top. Existing sourced financial comparisons remain available for the original guided professions.

The new reflections and their inference/ranking rules are editorial guidance. They need prospective student usability, counselor review and outcome evaluation; passing software tests and plausible fictional-model results do not establish student matching accuracy. No weak matches are added to fill slots, and a genuine lack of a confirmed Top or affordable entry-level option remains possible.
