## Financial preparation and calibration

All 50 additions have May 2025 Austin OEWS employee wage anchors and TWC entry proxies. No geographical fallback was needed for this wave. Mental-health counseling and personal care use explicitly broader combined wage groups. The entry proxy is the mean of the lowest-paid third, **not observed first-job pay**. Later 5/10/20-year figures are illustrations; three school-teaching profiles use the current AISD schedule example.

Full-time-equivalent wage figures are flagged for common part-time/seasonal roles. Self-employment, business revenue, commissions and irregular paid hours cannot be interpreted as guaranteed annual take-home pay. Managerial entry starts after related experience. Housing and spending remain adjustable budget assumptions, independent of interests.

Preparation sources distinguish typical education from legal minimums. Costs now lead with the applicable route: employer training; Austin paid academies; ACC credit/certificate examples; published program-cost sheets; or clearly labeled graduate references. Published undated estimates, partial fees, temporary advertised discounts, living-cost exclusions and aid eligibility stay attached to their amounts. Optional college study is described through concrete educational benefits; no salary premium is invented.

## Questionnaire changes and limits

The 30 Fieldguide prompts are original conversation prompts, with five activities per RIASEC area. The original 12 meanings/IDs remain intact;18 broaden practical care, repair, outdoor, service, performance, operations and customer work. Older drafts retain questionnaire provenance. These prompts are **not** a validated O*NET instrument, a performance test or an aptitude score.

The guided process is broad fields → selected fields → concrete day-to-day activities → careers → one plan. Task choices are balanced across selected fields and distinguish dentistry, medicine, pharmacy, rehabilitation, imaging, office and hands-on work. A literal selected task brings its career forward with an explicit reason. Unselected tasks remain unknown. Ties remain available; the student chooses the main career.

Custom broad-theme links use the mean of answered 1–5 responses within a theme, requiring at least three responses, two likes and mean ≥ 3.5. Unknowns are omitted. A fully covered profile spanning less than 0.5 in theme means gets no differentiated custom highlight. Health additionally weighs helping65% and investigation35%; science weighs investigation80% and practical work20%; engineering/building weighs practical work60% and investigation40%, requiring answered companion themes. This prevents a strong secondary theme alone from creating an implausible highlight. These are disclosed editorial heuristics, not statistical cutoffs or official O*NET matching. The old first 12-question shortcut was removed. Changing fields prunes unrelated task likes.

Confidence, self-reported experience, free text, ability reports, income and lifestyle remain separate from interest calculations. Low confidence with little exposure prompts a beginner-friendly action. Students may skip sections explicitly; unknowns do not become zeros and do not create return loops. Priorities accept one to three choices.

Official instrument imports preserve exact form, scale, source and raw/display separation. All six scores remain visible. Only the highest tied raw level is highlighted for editorial field discussion, avoiding a forced weak runner-up. Provider-returned occupations remain distinct; Fieldguide does not recreate official correlation rankings.
