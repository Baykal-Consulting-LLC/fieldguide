# Additional comparators: major selection and performance assessment

Research date: October 18, 2026. Public official sources only. These additional services complete the 27-service scope of the main review. No assessment was completed, account created, purchase made or student information submitted.

## MyMajors

The student-facing service advertises a free, approximately 15-minute assessment returning ten suggested majors, with career and college connections. Exact current item count was not verified. A useful distinction from a career-first guide is its immediate connection to majors. [Student product](https://www.mymajors.com/)

Its institutional explanation names courses, grades/GPA, interest in courses and work preferences as inputs; configurations can map recommendations to a subscribing institution's programs, with wider transfer options possible. These are academic records and self-report inputs, not evidence of a timed performance-ability battery. The reviewed pages do not disclose a reproducible current algorithm or representative high-school predictive validation. Vendor completion/retention claims were not independently verified and are not treated as a causal benefit. [Official input/configuration description](https://www.mymajors.com/highered/about.html)

**Implication for Fieldguide:** strengthen the connection from a career to two or three useful majors and real program routes. Do not collect full academic histories before giving students useful results, and do not let one institution's offerings silently define every possible path. Public pages were reviewed; authenticated report usability and full privacy arrangements remain unverified.

## Johnson O'Connor Research Foundation

Its FAQ describes a performance aptitude battery, knowledge/vocabulary and interest assessment, with personality context. It accepts students from age 14 and describes two in-person testing sessions of two to three hours each, followed by a 60–90-minute consultation. Current advertised fee is $950; the page also describes roughly five to six hours of testing overall, written/digital results and an optional follow-up consultation. Exact duration is therefore a range, not a single standardized app-session time. The consultant helps interpret patterns and possible careers. [Official FAQ](https://www.jocrf.org/testing/faq/)

Its research section and annual reports document ongoing work on norms, age/practice effects and test development. This review did not establish a prospective accuracy figure for an individual teenager's final career recommendations. Vendor statements about innate or stable abilities should not be repeated as universal developmental facts. Historical work does not automatically validate every later digital version. [Research](https://www.jocrf.org/research/), [technical reports](https://www.jocrf.org/research/technical-reports/)

**Implication for Fieldguide:** performance assessment supplies different information from interests and self-reported experience. It can remain an optional external resource for a family seeking that depth. An hours-long assessment and consultation should not become our required mobile journey. Accommodations, time, cost and interpretation matter alongside the assessment's name.

## ASVAB Career Exploration Program

The official ASVAB site describes a free program for participating schools that supports civilian and military career exploration. It combines ability testing with interest exploration and planning. Eligible scores can also be used in enlistment processes, subject to requirements; this is not merely an anonymous consumer interest quiz. [Official program overview](https://www.officialasvab.com/counselors-educators/asvab-career-exploration-program/)

The 2024 parent guide describes a separate 90-question Find Your Interests inventory and access through a student's score sheet. A newer official overview illustrates a 50-question version. We did not verify which version applies to every current school session and therefore do not present one universal count or transfer evidence between versions. The parent guide also explains school/recruiter score-release arrangements. [2024 parent guide](https://prod-media.asvabprogram.com/CEP_PDF_Contents/parents-guide-to-career-exploration.pdf), [official overview](https://prod-media.asvabprogram.com/CEP_PDF_Contents/ASVAB_CEP_Overview.pdf)

Post-test guidance connects score interpretation, interests, occupational information and career plans; this is a different scope from a single RIASEC questionnaire. The observed public documents support this structure, not an independently verified current recommendation-accuracy number. [Official post-test guide](https://prod-media.asvabprogram.com/CEP_PDF_Contents/ASVAB_CEP_Post_Test.pdf)

**Implication for Fieldguide:** borrow the idea of linking different kinds of evidence to an action plan, without suggesting that a self-rating is an ability score. Any external referral should explain purpose and score-sharing choices clearly. We did not administer tests, inspect student scores or evaluate a school implementation.

## Shared caution

These services answer different questions. A major-recommendation tool, performance battery, interest survey and school planning program should not be scored on a single invented accuracy scale. Compare the intended use, exact version, population, burden, interpretation and observed student benefit. No result above justifies adding hours of testing to Fieldguide.
