# Student decision and completion outcomes evidence addendum

Reviewed October 18, 2026. Scope: Xello, Naviance, Unifrog, MajorClarity, and Scoir. This supplements the public feature review with original studies and their limits; it does not rank the platforms' overall quality.

The evidence is more substantive than framework alignment alone, but it does not support a general claim that a platform makes each student choose a better career. Independent studies show that guidance interfaces can change actual education decisions, including unintended narrowing. Vendor reports more commonly measure planning activity, recorded completion, or adults' impressions. These outcomes must remain separate.

## Xello and the independent MDRC evaluation

**Independent comparison:** MDRC's August 2026 report pools Xello/YouScience implementations for grade-nine cohorts in three sites. Pandemic disruption replaced planned randomization with matching: 2,090 matched students/16 schools; 1,744 transcript records. The primary attempted-CTE-concentration difference was not significant. Attempted credits and one young-women subgroup comparison were negative. Controls sometimes used other tools, implementation varied, follow-up survey response was inadequate, and confounding remains possible. No isolated current-product effect or individual career-match accuracy was established. [Final report, chapters 1–3](https://www.mdrc.org/work/publications/lessons-technology-assisted-advising-model-career-and-technical-education/file-full).

**What the newer positive marketing metrics measure:** Xello commissioned Hanover's 2026 *Return on Impact* study. It combines a winter survey of 357 US educators/district leaders who use Xello, 12 spring interviews, and August 2025–June 2026 platform activity. The 78% “positive impact on career-plan completion” is an **adult respondent judgment**, not 78% of students completing plans, a measured improvement, or a student survey result. There is no randomized or matched nonuser comparison in this report. Selection, recall, and favorable-user-response risks remain. Importantly, Xello's landing page calls the respondents “students,” while the original report consistently identifies educators/leaders; use the report's denominator. [Hanover report, pp. 5–6 and 16](https://xello.world/wp-content/uploads/Xello-Hanover_ReturnOnImpactReport_2026.pdf), [Xello landing page with discrepant wording](https://xello.world/en/all-in-one-solution/).

**Interpretation:** current educator satisfaction evidence and a restricted historical comparison study can coexist. Neither establishes personalized career-match prediction accuracy. The finding most useful for Fieldguide is to measure whether students understand and act on guidance, rather than assuming saved plans or survey satisfaction prove better decisions.

## Naviance and actual college application decisions

**Independent peer-reviewed evidence exists.** Christine Mulhern's *Changing College Choices with Personalized Admissions Information at Scale: Evidence on Naviance* appeared online in 2020 and in *Journal of Labor Economics* in 2021. Its author-posted April 2019 working version analyzes one district and exploits scattergram visibility thresholds and variation in displayed admissions averages using regression discontinuity. It measures applications and college attendance. The published abstract reports shifts toward colleges with visible schoolmate information, greater responses among disadvantaged students, increased four-year attendance from in-state information, and deterrence from selective colleges. This is evidence about historical information access/framing, not validation of all Naviance modules or the current design. The working paper is not silently treated as the final published specification. [Published article and abstract](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/708839), [author-posted working paper](https://papers.cmulhern.com/Naviance_Mulhern_April2019.pdf).

Tomkins, Grossman, Page, and Goel's 2023 PNAS paper examines 70,900 Common App applicants at 220 public high schools using variation in adoption timing, within-school before/after comparisons, and adjusted regressions. Among approximately 17,000 highly competitive applicants, it finds more application undermatching: the unadjusted comparison rises from about 15% to 24%; adjusted odds ratios are 2.1–2.2. This is not a randomized trial or a representative national sample. The outcome is applying only to less-selective colleges under the authors' academic-competitiveness definition; it does not measure enrollment, graduation, career satisfaction, or wages. The study concerns historical scattergrams, not a current-form product trial. [*Showing high-achieving college applicants past admissions outcomes increases undermatching*, original article, pp. 2–5](https://5harad.com/papers/undermatching.pdf).

**Administrative completion is a different evidence class.** Philadelphia's Office of Research and Evaluation audited 2016/17 and 2017/18 platform records. In 2017/18, 34,469 high-school students received at least one task; Career Interest Profiler completion was 26.4% of 9,141 assigned tenth graders, and the recorded FAFSA task completion was 48.3% of 7,813 assigned seniors. The report explicitly discourages comparing the years because task requirements changed. There is no untreated comparison identifying a platform benefit. These are useful implementation/drop-off observations, not intrinsic quiz-completion rates or proof that Naviance improved FAFSA submission. [District research report, pp. 3 and 8](https://www.philasd.org/research/wp-content/uploads/sites/90/2019/02/Naviance-Usage-2016-17-and-2017-18-Research-Report-Feb-2019.pdf).

**Implication:** do not equate confidence, more applications, or following a recommendation with a better decision. Test whether concise explanations inadvertently discourage viable options or anchor students on a single threshold.

## MajorClarity and the 189 percent headline

**Original vendor evidence:** the linked *ESSA Evidence-Based Impact Study* describes 2,214 active users in one district with one middle school and one high school. Exposure is completing a career test-drive; outcome is selecting, or having selected, a career-aligned course plan. It reports an odds ratio of 2.890 (95% CI 2.398–3.486), the source of “189%.” That is an association in **odds**, not a 189% probability gain. Allocation is not randomized; the displayed regression lists only test-drive completion, with shared district context offered as control for other influences. Motivation, staff encouragement, and prior planning can confound the association. Dates, exact grades, product version, group counts, and temporal ordering are not sufficiently disclosed. Its stated 94% versus 63% completion rates imply a crude odds ratio near 9.2, inconsistent with the reported single-predictor 2.89; the report does not resolve this. It measures plan selection, not courses passed, credentials, employment, or later career fit. [Original report, visually inspected pp. 4–6](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6050d46fe122839adc807f0c/6050d46fe12283220780802f_MajorClarity%20ESSA%20Evidence%20Impact%20Study.pdf), [official report link](https://majorclarity.com/case-studies).

**Interpretation:** the study is useful as a hypothesis that experiential exploration may relate to planning follow-through. It does not isolate a causal test-drive effect or establish an independently verified ESSA evidence tier. Obtain a clarified analysis before repeating its effect size as an outcome benefit.

## Unifrog and opportunities completed or explored

The current **2024/25 Impact Report** describes 2.2+ million students and 4,000+ schools, 22,300+ student attendances at 90 virtual events, and 8,800+ Year 12/13 students receiving tailored opportunities. These are reach/exposure measures; no comparison group or longitudinal decision-quality estimate is supplied. [2024/25 Impact Report](https://cdn.unifrog.org/downloads/Unifrog_Impact_Report_202425.pdf).

The **2023/24 report** describes 200,000+ placements facilitated through 1,600 schools/colleges, a substantive operational output. Its Schroders event example reports a 467% rise in apprenticeship shortlisting after an insight day, but gives neither counts nor an untreated group or adjustment for timing and promotion. Shortlisting is not apprenticeship application, acceptance, or completion. [2023/24 Impact Report, pp. 6–8](https://cdn.unifrog.org/downloads/23_24_Impact_report.pdf).

The **2022/23 report** says 94% of surveyed webinar participants reported greater understanding and claims 4,000 students were prevented from becoming NEET. The short report does not supply survey response counts/rates or a counterfactual method for the prevented-NEET estimate. Do not treat that number as an estimated causal effect. [2022/23 Impact Report, pp. 5–7](https://cdn.unifrog.org/downloads/Impact-of-Unifrog-report-2223_1.pdf). Six-month psychometric reviews concern instrument consistency; they do not establish downstream destination improvement. [Psychometric testing FAQs](https://www.unifrog.org/help/unifrogs-psychometric-testing-faqs).

**Interpretation:** public reports provide concrete exposure, placement administration, and self-reported learning evidence. A controlled improvement in student decisions or destinations remains unverified in the reviewed reports; that is a bounded finding, not a claim that no other study exists.

## Scoir and recorded application activity

The official **Miami-Dade case study** covers onboarding 84 traditional public high schools in March 2020 and approximately 20,000 class-of-2022 students. As of March 2022 it reports 89% registered accounts, 54% active applications, and 22.5% of the senior class reporting an accepted outcome. The text and infographic differ on whether the 54% denominator is registered students or all seniors. October 2023 class-of-2024 activity includes 85% registered and 74% logging in within 60 days. There is no untreated or pre-adoption outcome comparison, no adjustment for the district's College Assistance Program/support, and no isolation of an assessment effect. The figures describe recorded adoption/application activity at particular dates, not improved application completion or enrollment caused by Scoir. [Miami-Dade case study, pp. 7–9](https://www.scoir.com/hubfs/Success%20Story%20-%20Miami-Dade%20County%20Public%20Schools%20(1).pdf?hsLang=en-us).

Current Admission Intelligence documentation explains predictive inputs, including GPA/test scores and school/context data, and excludes course rigor and extracurricular activities at the documented version. Prediction from historical outcomes is not evidence that using the prediction improves outcomes. A controlled student decision/completion study or public current calibration table was not verified in this focused review. [Introducing Admission Intelligence, updated November 2025](https://www.scoir.com/whats-new-in-scoir/introducing-admission-intelligence).

## Review of the main synthesis

Reviewed `site/research/competitive-services-review-2026-10-18.md`. No material factual error was identified in its school-platform question/time/access rows; they appropriately distinguish estimates, separate modules, and public-document evidence. Its 27-service count matches the table. This review did not re-audit its non-school products or local application implementation.

The material gap is **outcome-evidence coverage**, rather than the recommended friendly product direction:

- Add the final MDRC study to the accuracy discussion near its current line 105. It supersedes relying on Xello's study announcement. Describe a matched, pooled, implementation-limited comparison, not an RCT demonstrating ineffectiveness.
- Add the independent Naviance decision studies. “Inspectability” and school adoption do not guarantee beneficial effects; the empirical framing/anchoring warning is directly relevant to displaying limited recommendations and fit thresholds.
- If reporting MajorClarity's 189% claim anywhere, state that it is a vendor-reported odds association with unresolved reporting limitations. The current synthesis prudently omits the headline and should retain that restraint.
- Do not import the Xello landing page's “78% of students” wording. The original Hanover report identifies adult respondents. Do not reinterpret Unifrog's internal-consistency reviews as evidence of improved destinations.

Keep the formative pilot, independent review, optional-depth investigation, and 4–6-week follow-up proposed in the synthesis. Add two outcome checks: **did the student complete a relevant next action**, and **can they explain viable alternatives without treating the tool's first suggestion as a verdict**? For any comparison, freeze versions, define the outcome before testing, preserve noncompletion in the denominator, and distinguish better recording from better behavior. These are proposed evaluation safeguards, not validation already achieved.
