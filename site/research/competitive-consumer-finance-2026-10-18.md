# Consumer career guidance and financial planning benchmarks

Research date: October 18, 2026. Audience: a high-school student and parent using a short, friendly career-exploration app.

The most useful combination is a short interest-led start, optional work-values refinement, understandable reasons for several career suggestions, and a separate local money scenario. None of the reviewed public evidence justifies treating a proprietary match score as the probability that a teenager will succeed in a career. The money view should compare clearly labeled wage distributions with an editable budget, then connect the student to education, training and financial-aid next steps.

## Evidence and access boundaries

- **Documented** means official vendor, government, institution or research documentation read through public web retrieval. Vendor claims remain attributed claims. Published time estimates are not measured completion times.
- **Observed** is limited to Princeton Review's anonymous browser start screen: the visible session displayed the legacy 24-question form and its first four questions without sign-in. No answers were submitted. Other products were reviewed through public pages, FAQ text and sample reports; their complete flows and paid results were not tested.
- No accounts, purchases, real student answers or financial details were supplied. No private data or school license codes were used. Publicly available price text establishes advertised pricing, not a completed checkout price.
- MyPlan's canonical pages repeatedly timed out in direct web retrieval. Its official indexed pages supplied the quoted counts, timings and prices; the values page also appeared on an institutional MyPlan subdomain. Current operation of those assessments is unverified. College Scorecard and College Navigator landing pages also failed direct retrieval; their official documentation, rather than an invented live walkthrough, supports the comparisons below.

## What the evidence supports about matching

**PathwayU provides the strongest publicly explained research chain in this subset**, but the distinction between construct validity, an intervention outcome and the accuracy of a particular ranked recommendation still matters. Its report discusses meta-analyses and an earlier-software randomized study involving over 600 students; outcomes were confidence, decision difficulty and related career-development measures. A benefit from software plus a 90-minute workshop does not demonstrate that a 20-minute current assessment can forecast an individual teenager's earnings or job satisfaction. [PathwayU science report](https://www.peoplegrove.com/resource-library/the-science-behind-pathwayu/).

CareerExplorer's machine-learning/large-response-count claims and CareerFitter's calculation counts describe their methods or scale, not a published error rate. CareerFitter is unusually explicit about its unavailable technical statistics. For this app, use “suggested paths to explore,” a few response-based reasons, and room for disagreement. An 87% match should never be presented as an 87% chance of success without an actual probability target and out-of-sample calibration. These are interpretation and design conclusions drawn from the limits of the reviewed evidence. [CareerExplorer explanation](https://www.careerexplorer.com/career-test/), [CareerFitter limitations](https://www.careerfitter.com/about-careerfitter/research-development).

Avoid turning preference descriptions into fixed ability judgments. MyPlan explicitly distinguishes its skills self-assessment from aptitude; Truity's sample uses categorical strengths/challenges language and advises avoiding some tasks. A high-school student may have had little exposure to those tasks. Better follow-up: “Try a small project or talk with someone in this job,” then update the exploration list. [MyPlan skills definition](https://www.myplan.com/assess/skills.php), [Truity sample](https://www.truity.com/sites/default/files/cpprofiler_sample.html).

### Texas Reality Check assumptions worth teaching explicitly

The FAQ says apartment rent uses HUD fair-market rents; home payments use area median sale prices, a 30-year mortgage and **20% down**; insurance assumes a healthy adult in their twenties; student debt uses average yearly borrowing, with **20-year repayment for most degrees and 30 years for professional degrees**. Those are modeling choices, not personal quotes or necessarily the student's actual loan terms. The FAQ's family-cost explanation uses USDA child-cost estimates. [Texas Reality Check FAQ](https://texasrealitycheck.com/faq).

Transfer the approachable spending-choice interaction, but expose consequential assumptions beside the result. A small monthly debt payment created by a long repayment term can conceal higher lifetime interest. A home-payment scenario can conceal a large up-front down payment. Verify current sources and dates before reusing any default; the reviewed FAQ says “current” without giving a date for each input. A 2024 TWC budget request proposes user-entered expenses, Spanish and a self-sufficiency wage, but a proposal is not evidence those features shipped. [FAQ](https://texasrealitycheck.com/faq), [TWC request, page 6](https://www.twc.texas.gov/sites/default/files/ogc/mtg24/commission-meeting-material-082024-item13b-commission-exceptional-item-summary-fy26-27-twc.pdf).

## Transferable product ideas

The following are design recommendations derived from the comparisons, not claims that a short custom quiz has been validated.

1. **Start with one short module and an honest progress count.** Aim for a friendly initial session around 5–10 minutes, subject to actual student testing. Show useful suggestions before offering optional values/preferences refinement. Do not require MyPlan's full four-test burden or CareerExplorer's longer full profile to see a first result.
2. **Explain several paths, including trades.** Show a small set of varied options with “why this appeared,” a task the student might enjoy, a possible friction point and a short experiment. Use separate education-route cards for certificate, apprenticeship, associate and bachelor's pathways where appropriate; confirm each real path from its provider.
3. **Make financial fit a second lens.** Keep personal interest and affordability visible separately. A student's desired spending should not silently rewrite their interests. Let them compare “this career with a smaller budget” and “this budget with other careers.” Texas Reality Check provides the useful two-way concept.
4. **Use an editable monthly scenario.** Begin with location, living arrangement and household size, then housing, transport and discretionary choices. Expand details only when requested. Show gross wage → modeled taxes/deductions → take-home → expenses → savings/debt → remaining margin. Separate recurring costs from deposits, down payments and training costs.
5. **Preserve financial definitions.** On salary cards show occupation, geography, year, median and available wage distribution. Label any lower-wage scenario as a scenario; a percentile is not a starting-pay claim. Do not derive a five-year salary trajectory from cross-sectional wage percentiles. Identify modeled taxes, hours, benefits, aid and loan terms explicitly.
6. **Show tradeoffs before precision.** Round monthly results to useful amounts and show lower/base/higher scenarios or a sensitivity slider. Explain when rent, hours, household size or debt changes the conclusion. Do not invent statistical confidence intervals from a budget range. Missing data should produce “not available” plus a broader-geography fallback label.
7. **End with three concrete actions.** Save two careers to compare, try one relevant experience, and check two education routes using their actual costs/net-price calculators. An optional parent discussion view should invite reflection; it should not turn a student's profile into a fixed label.
8. **Keep privacy and paywalls clear before effort.** Truity's no-email basic-result approach is worth adapting; disclose what remains free at the beginning, not after dozens of answers. Make save/share optional and explain who can see a minor's results. Public privacy statements were not technically audited here; no vendor should be labeled “privacy safe” solely from them.

## Calibration and verification before shipping a money view

For a product implementation, keep the exact public-data vintage and geographic key on every input, and save the household/scenario assumptions with the result. Validate calculations against a small set of official reference examples and independent recomputation; do not use competitors' attractive output as ground truth. Test zero/part-time hours, missing wages, roommates, multiple earners, no children, tuition versus total attendance cost, aid versus loans, and overlapping child/housing/medical categories. A baseline imported from MIT or Texas Reality Check must not be added to a second set of the same expenses.

Use a loan's actual principal, rate and term for the displayed scenario, with both monthly payment and total repayment. Keep college-study years and post-training work years separate and do not subtract tuition again from a wage that is already being shown after a loan payment. Label an assumed wage or inflation path as an assumption. Test these distinctions with students and parents: can they explain what the number includes and change one choice to improve the outcome? That is a more meaningful usability target than making the dashboard display an exact-looking dollar amount.

Unresolved commercial/research details requiring vendor documentation rather than inference: current PathwayU item counts and individual pricing; all proprietary scoring equations and teen-specific prospective calibration; Truity's current consumer checkout price; CareerExplorer's legacy membership discrepancy; MyPlan's live compatibility and source vintages; Princeton Review's rollout/version behavior and final result gate; Texas Reality Check's current tax/debt/cost vintage and completed-flow behavior. No ranking of actual predictive accuracy is justified by this public review.
