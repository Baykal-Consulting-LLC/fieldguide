# Assessment-led career guidance comparators

Research date: **October 18, 2026** (client date). Scope: public official product/support pages, technical reports, and sample reports; no purchase, signup, student-data submission, or completed assessment. UX statements below describe published workflows, not hands-on usability testing. Prices are public-page observations, not checkout quotations. Undated sources were retrieved on the research date; search-engine crawl/publication estimates were not treated as document dates.

## Decision-relevant findings

- The closest transparent, low-burden benchmark is the official O*NET Interest Profiler family. Its 30-item Mini-IP is a separately developed form, not permission to drop half the existing 60 questions or rewrite their wording. Preserve the existing unchanged 60-item/five-label implementation unless deliberately adopting the official Mini-IP and its scoring, provenance, and limitations. See the comparative technical evidence below.
- Separate **interest**, **confidence in skills**, **performance on ability tasks**, and **work values** in both labels and explanations. Kuder explicitly distinguishes these; CareerOneStop offers separate tools. Combining them invisibly into one “aptitude” percentage would obscure what was actually measured. [Kuder student-side guide, undated](https://www.kuder.com/get-started-navigator/explore-student-side/); [CareerOneStop career tools, undated](https://cloudfront.careeronestop.org/Toolkit/Careers/careers.aspx).
- YouScience (~90 minutes), Morrisby (~80–110 minutes by configuration), and Highlands (~3 hours plus consultation) offer depth but conflict with an immediate, short-survey promise. Their useful transferable ideas are modular sessions, understandable strength descriptions, editable exploration, and human discussion—not mandatory replication of their entire batteries. [YouScience Discovery FAQs, undated](https://www.youscience.com/discovery-faqs/); [Morrisby administration guide, updated August 11, 2026](https://support.morrisby.com/support/solutions/articles/44002054625-administering-morrisby-profile-assessments); [Highlands method, undated](https://www.highlandsco.com/the-method/).
- Strong now has a public **244-item** technical supplement dated April 21, 2026. Older pages still describe 291 items and 130 occupations. Version identification matters; the supplement reports 321 occupation and 177 academic-major similarity scales. [Strong 244 technical supplement](https://strongsupplement.themyersbriggs.com/).

## 1. O*NET Interest Profiler / My Next Move — public benchmark

**What it measures and burden.** Six RIASEC interests; it asks preferences, not scored demonstrations of ability. Current resource page lists the web version as the **30-item Mini-IP**, the paper Short Form as **60 items**, free use, English/Spanish web access, API/widget integration, and links to over 900 occupations. Its general 10–20-minute estimate is less specific than the manual's form-specific times. Do not repeat older claims that the current public My Next Move web tool necessarily has 60 items. [Interest Profiler resource page, undated/current retrieval](https://www.onetcenter.org/IP.html).

**Age, timing, scoring and limits.** The March 2021 manual recommends approximately **14+**, approximately eighth-grade reading, and sufficient familiarity with work activities. It documents **Mini-IP 3–15 minutes** and **60-item web Short Form 5–20 minutes**, with additional exploration time. Computerized responses are summed by RIASEC area; profile correspondence with occupations uses correlations, with Job Zones indicating preparation requirements. The manual encourages retakes and multiple sources of guidance. It supports exploration rather than a guaranteed career assignment. This recommended lower age is not a claim of equally strong validation at every adolescent age. [O*NET Interest Profiler Manual, March 2021, chapter 3 pp.30–33 and chapter 2 pp.22–25](https://www.onetcenter.org/dl_files/IP_Manual.pdf).

**30 versus 60: actual evidence.** A separate development sample contained 1,061 participants; validation used 575 MTurk adults aged 18–65 with the shorter items embedded in the full inventory. Internal consistency was .74–.81 for Mini-IP versus .85–.90 for Short Form. This is adult evidence with overlapping items, not proof of equivalent teen outcomes; the longer form remains useful. [Mini-IP report, March 2016](https://www.onetcenter.org/dl_files/Mini-IP.pdf).

**60-item original validation.** The February 2010 Short Form report used a mixed development sample of **1,061**, collected at employment services, high schools, colleges, trade schools, universities and government agencies, and a separate **132-person stability sample**. It reports developmental alpha **.78–.87**, test-retest **.78–.86**, and convergent/discriminant evidence against Interest Finder. High-school inclusion provides adolescent relevance but is not a separate representative adolescent validation. Those numbers concern the original development/scoring conditions; do not treat them as estimates for every redesigned interface or altered item set. [Short Form Psychometric Characteristics: Summary, February 2010, pp.4–6](https://www.onetcenter.org/dl_files/IPSF_Psychometric.pdf).

**Outputs and explanation.** My Next Move occupational pages are the useful follow-through benchmark: e.g., civil engineers have plain-language tasks, knowledge/skills/abilities, education, outlook, pay, and training/job-search links. These describe occupations; they do not establish a student's admissions probability, affordability, or future earnings. [My Next Move: Civil Engineers, undated/current occupational profile](https://www.mynextmove.org/profile/summary/17-2051.00).

**Licensing.** Current license guidance distinguishes verbatim redistribution under **CC BY-ND 4.0** (credit and link; no modified redistribution) from modification under the **O*NET Tools Developer License**, which requires validation of products incorporating the content and a modification/nonendorsement notice. Developer registration is optional. The occupational database license is separate. Review actual implementation and attribution against the license; do not casually reword items or official response labels for friendliness. [O*NET Career Exploration Tools Content License, undated/current retrieval](https://www.onetcenter.org/license_tools.html).

**Transferable idea (researcher's recommendation).** Give truthful time ranges, progress, optional session recovery, clear RIASEC descriptions, and a short “why this appears” explanation. Improve the surrounding UI while retaining the chosen official form intact. Do not claim that a five-minute interest read measures innate aptitude or predicts college success.

## 2. CareerOneStop — public multi-tool benchmark

**Constructs and burden.** The official tool index describes a **30-question Interest Assessment**. The Skills Matcher asks **40 self-ratings** of current skill/knowledge with example anchors; these are self-assessments rather than performance tests. The Work Values Matcher ranks **20 statements**, producing six work-value priorities. Official time estimates for all three were not verified in the successfully retrieved pages; do not use third-party “five minutes” or “ten minutes” estimates as verified product facts. [Career tools, undated](https://cloudfront.careeronestop.org/Toolkit/Careers/careers.aspx); [CareerOneStop official Skills Matcher API repository, undated](https://github.com/CareerOneStop/Skills-Matcher); [CareerOneStop change-occupation user guide, undated PDF](https://cloudfront.careeronestop.org/TridionMultimedia/tcm24-50399_PDF_changeoccupationuserguideACC.pdf).

**How recommendations work.** Work Values help says a statistical formula compares the rankings with occupation value ratings; it links to O*NET's Work Importance Locator and warns that the underlying values data are no longer supported on O*NET. The Skills API confirms analysis into occupation matches but does not disclose its full formula in the reviewed README. Exact interest-matching implementation was not verified; do not assume it is identical to every O*NET web implementation merely because the construct is RIASEC. [Work Values Matcher Help, undated/current page](https://cloudfront.careeronestop.org/Toolkit/Careers/work-values-matcher-help.aspx); [Skills Matcher official repository](https://github.com/CareerOneStop/Skills-Matcher).

**Outputs/access/evidence.** Occupation profiles cover national/state wage, employment, education/training, and KSAs. The public workflow links assessment results to exploration. Several live pages returned automated-access 403 errors; this is a research-access limitation, not evidence that students face the same barrier. Public official material inspected did not establish a particular minimum age, adolescent norm sample, or validation of a combined three-tool recommendation. O*NET evidence should not be described as validating every CareerOneStop UI or formula. [Career tools](https://cloudfront.careeronestop.org/Toolkit/Careers/careers.aspx).

**Transferable idea.** Offer interests first and values/skills as explicitly optional follow-ups. Show occupation pay and education alongside fit, and explain that self-rated existing skill can disadvantage beginners in unfamiliar activities. Do not turn limited work experience into a ceiling on future learning potential.

## 3. YouScience Aptitude & Career Discovery

**Audience, tasks, and time.** The full Discovery FAQ identifies **grade 9 and beyond**, **11 exercises lasting 4–15 minutes**, approximately **90 minutes**, recommending two or more sessions; completed exercises save. This counts exercises, not every stimulus/question inside them. Total item count is not publicly disclosed in the reviewed current source. [Discovery FAQs, undated](https://www.youscience.com/discovery-faqs/).

**Access, output, and claims.** The individual purchase page publicly lists **US$49** for one assessment, a personalized **40-page** report, aptitude/interest career filters and bookmarks. It describes strengths, interpersonal style, work approach, timeframe orientation and verbal skills, and markets the matching as AI-based. Those are vendor descriptions; “brain wiring,” “best-fitting,” and “careers where you'll thrive” are claims, not measured guarantees. A verified school-license price was not found. College admissions and individualized college affordability outputs were not established by this individual package page. [Buy Aptitude & Career Discovery, undated/current retrieval](https://www.youscience.com/buy-now/).

**Technical evidence versus current version.** A vendor-hosted report by HumRRO's Rodney McCloy and Dan Putka describes an augmented Ball Aptitude Battery, a **60-item O*NET interest measure**, O*NET ability/interest mappings, comparable scaling, similarity algorithms, and simulated-user vetting. It reports gender invariance and partial racial/ethnic invariance. The exact matching coefficients and complete norm-sample demographics are not disclosed there. Its **15-dimension/older battery description differs from today's 11-exercise marketing**, so current-version equivalence requires confirmation. The report draws substantially on foundational aptitude research; this is not an independent prospective trial showing current Discovery recommendations improve adolescent career outcomes. Publication date is not clearly shown; references include retrievals in 2014. [The Validity Behind YouScience, undated PDF, pp.1–7](https://resources.youscience.com/rs/806-BFU-539/images/Validity_Full_Report.pdf).

**UX and safety.** Only public workflow reviewed. Session saving is attractive; ~90 minutes is still substantial. Avoid importing deterministic “hard-wired” wording into student guidance. Ask for current adolescent norms, accommodations, version mapping, reliability by scale, and prospective outcome evidence before stronger claims.

## 4. Morrisby Profile

**Audience and battery.** The August 2026 administration guide specifies **15+/UK Year 10+**, verbal, numerical, abstract, spatial, mechanical, interests and personality, with optional priorities/study interests. Full configuration is **about 1 hour 50 minutes**; abridged is **about 80 minutes**, omitting spatial, mechanical and personality until later. Total question count was not located publicly. [Administration Guide, updated August 11, 2026](https://support.morrisby.com/support/solutions/articles/44002054625-administering-morrisby-profile-assessments).

**Time discrepancies and barriers.** Parent marketing says ~90 minutes; older parent support says ~100; current at-home guidance says up to two hours. These are configuration/source differences, not one precise universal completion time. At-home instructions say PC/tablet/iPad, no phone; timed tasks, quiet conditions, resume after closing browser, and possible school-controlled release of results. [Parents page, undated](https://www.morrisby.com/parents); [Parent support, updated July 25, 2025](https://support.morrisby.com/support/solutions/articles/44002058084-morrisby-profile-information-for-parents); [At-home guide, updated October 2, 2026](https://support.morrisby.com/support/solutions/articles/44002273706-morrisby-profile-taking-morrisby-profile-at-home).

**Matching, explanation, outputs.** Morrisby says recommendations combine abilities, interests, personality and priorities; its history page describes a fuzzy-logic expert system. That is high-level documentation, not disclosed coefficients or a reproducible current scoring specification. Parents can be invited by the student; subject, course, apprenticeship, college/university and application guidance plus goals are advertised. “Not unduly influenced by educational or social background” is a vendor claim, not evidence of fairness across every group. [Morrisby Profile](https://www.morrisby.com/morrisby-profile); [About/history](https://www.morrisby.com/about); [Parents](https://www.morrisby.com/parents).

**Verified public price.** UK individual ages 15–18: **£96 including VAT**, Profile/lifetime login/30-minute careers meeting. Over 18: £132 with 60-minute meeting. School and international packages differ; do not compare the £25 school credit price with the individual counseling package as though equivalent. [Pricing, undated/current retrieval](https://www.morrisby.com/pricing).

**Evidence gap.** The official pages inspected did not yield a current public technical manual with reliability, adolescent norm demographics, SEM, subgroup fairness statistics, or evidence for the recommendation engine's outcomes. This is a disclosure gap in this review, not proof that such evidence does not exist. Published historical use and BPS-registration statements are not substitutes for those details. Transfer the student-controlled parent invitation and what-if exploration; avoid making the entire lengthy battery mandatory.

## 5. Kuder Navigator

**Audience, constructs, burden.** Designed for **grades 6–12**. Current student-side guide distinguishes interests, **skills confidence** (self-perceived ability), and 12 work values, advertising all three within **20 minutes or less**. Its 2019 KCIA-L technical brief specifies **60 interest items**. Current total counts for the confidence and values instruments were not verified. A separate June 2025 Journey help page estimates interest assessment at about ten minutes; Journey is not identical to Navigator. [Navigator student-side guide](https://www.kuder.com/get-started-navigator/explore-student-side/); [KCIA-L Technical Brief, August 1, 2019](https://www.kuder.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/KCIA-L-2019-Technical-Brief.pdf); [Journey help, June 19, 2025](https://help.kuder.com/en/articles/9773934-users-how-to-take-career-assessments-in-kuder-journey).

**Documented matching and evidence.** The KCIA-L brief says six Holland scores match O*NET occupations through a **Euclidean-distance similarity index**; cluster/pathway profiles use constituent-occupation median scores. It reports student input, analysis of 3,194 U.S. students, alpha **.907–.950**, CFA RMSEA **.068**, and DIF checks. Its reliability table labels a smaller **N=2,045**, an internal reporting inconsistency worth clarifying before quoting a single normative N. This is vendor technical evidence for that interest version, not independent proof of long-term outcomes or validation of every platform component. [2019 technical brief](https://www.kuder.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/KCIA-L-2019-Technical-Brief.pdf).

**Outputs/access.** Navigator describes occupations, majors, course plans, schools, financial aid/scholarship search and portfolio favorites. School activation/login is required; exact institutional price was not publicly verified. English/Spanish and grade-specific progression are advertised. These are official feature descriptions, not observed result quality. [Navigator product page](https://www.kuder.com/education-solutions/navigator/); [Login/register](https://navigator.kuder.com/account/login-register?ReturnURL=%2Fkuder-career-interests-assessment); [My Next Steps help, August 11, 2026](https://help.kuder.com/en/articles/9757816-students-how-to-use-my-next-steps).

**Transferable idea.** A small “next step” list and favorites can turn results into action. Keep confidence separate from measured aptitude, and show which assessment contributed to a suggestion. Outcome-study titles in the vendor library alone do not establish study quality; they were not independently evaluated here.

## 6. Strong Interest Inventory — The Myers-Briggs Company

**Version and burden.** Strong is an interest inventory, distinct from MBTI personality typing. Strong 244 launched May 2023 and coexists with the earlier form. It has **244 items**; reviewed current sources did not establish a definitive standard completion time or minimum age specifically for 244. Do not silently attach the older product's 15+ age guidance or a 291-item timing to 244. [All about Strong 244, launch described May 2023](https://www.themyersbriggs.com/en-US/Access-Resources/Articles/all-about-the-new-strong-244-career-and-interest-assessment); [Strong 244 technical supplement, April 21, 2026](https://strongsupplement.themyersbriggs.com/).

**Constructs/computation.** General Occupational Themes cover RIASEC; Basic Interest and Personal Style scales add detail. The supplement documents IRT scoring for broad/basic interests, gender-neutral scoring and administrative response checks. Occupation similarity scales use **elastic-net penalized logistic regression**, comparing occupation samples with a general reference sample; they are similarity scores, not probabilities of admission or success. Major similarity is also reported. [Technical supplement introduction](https://strongsupplement.themyersbriggs.com/); [Occupation Similarity Scales, chapter 5](https://strongsupplement.themyersbriggs.com/chapter5.html).

**Calibration and evidence.** The general reference sample is **100,000 U.S. working adults aged at least 18**, mean **36.5**, at least somewhat satisfied with their work. This is not a representative adolescent norm. GOT alpha is **.84–.91**, with short retest **.79–.87** and longer retest **.73–.88**. These support score consistency, not guaranteed decisions. The current supplement provides unusually inspectable technical detail, but remains publisher-authored; independent adolescent decision-outcome validation was not identified in this review. [Supplement introduction](https://strongsupplement.themyersbriggs.com/); [General Occupational Themes, reliability Table 3.2](https://strongsupplement.themyersbriggs.com/chapter2.html).

**Explanation/access/output.** Public sample reports make interest areas and career exploration readable; access is mediated through qualified practitioners/assessment services. Exact consumer all-in price was not verified. No individualized college cost/aid calculation was established. [Strong product page and sample-report links](https://www.themyersbriggs.com/en-US/Products-and-Services/Strong); [244 sample profile](https://www.themyersbriggs.com/-/media/Myers-Briggs/Files/Sample-Reports/Strong-244-Profile-Sample-Report.pdf).

**Transferable idea.** Explain broad interests and more specific areas, invite the student to reflect on agreement/disagreement, and avoid presenting a model score as a forecast. Do not reproduce licensed items or normative scoring tables as app content.

## 7. Highlands Ability Battery (HAB)

**Audience/tasks/burden.** Official method page describes students/adults, **18 abilities plus vocabulary**, timed performance worksamples, and approximately **three hours** at home. Homepage describes **19 timed exercises**; that is a task count, not total item count. Current numerical minimum age was not verified; historical research includes a 15–21 norm group. [Method](https://www.highlandsco.com/the-method/); [Homepage](https://www.highlandsco.com/).

**Access/explanation/output.** Reports list potential jobs and roles; a certified-consultant debrief is part of the method, and Highlands explicitly says it does not offer individuals the HAB without that consultant. Values, interests and experience enter the broader consultation. No uniform current all-in price, college finder, or individualized affordability output was verified. The public method does not provide a reproducible career-matching formula. [Method, undated/current retrieval](https://www.highlandsco.com/the-method/).

**Technical evidence and limitations.** Highlands publishes a **2002 contracted Chauncey Group statistical audit** of its CD-ROM version: **4,307 records**, ages 15–21/22–30/31–39/40+, 19 assignments yielding 23 scores. It explicitly labels its reliability discussion “indirect” because speeded tests require special studies; it also flags a vocabulary ceiling concern. This is stronger than a testimonial but is a paid historical audit, not independent validation of today's online battery or career recommendations. Do not translate intercorrelations into a current test-retest coefficient. [A Report on the Statistical Characteristics of the Highlands Ability Battery, 2002, pp.1–3](https://www.highlandsco.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/9-A-Report-on-the-Statistical-Characteristics.pdf).

**Transferable idea.** Contextualized human interpretation can prevent deterministic reading of a long report. Its hours-long battery and consultant requirement make it a different service category from an immediate friendly website. “Born with” and stable-after-14 claims on the vendor research page should not be repeated as universal developmental facts. [HAB Technology and Research, undated](https://www.highlandsco.com/whats-highlands-ability-battery/hab-technology-and-research/).

## Cross-service evidence and product guardrails

These are research recommendations, not claims about a competitor's implementation:

1. Present results as hypotheses for exploration; give several plausible paths and a concrete next experience (course, club, interview, shadowing), with space for disagreement.
2. Show the construct and reason behind a match. Interest similarity, self-confidence, timed-task performance, labor-market demand and affordability answer different questions.
3. Use a chosen validated instrument intact. To reduce friction, improve navigation, saving, instructions and surrounding explanations; shortening/rewording requires deliberate form/version selection and new evidence where applicable.
4. Treat rankings as uncertain; small score differences should not appear to determine a teenager's future. No reviewed public package establishes a probability that one particular student will succeed in a college or career.
5. Make optional modules optional. Keep the first result useful; do not gate it behind every available survey. Published completion time is not a UX performance measurement on this website.
6. Separate career facts from student measurement: pay/outlook and education are occupation data; admissions, aid, family budget and net price need separate current data and assumptions.
7. Before relying on any proprietary battery for adolescent decisions, request its exact version, age/language norms, accommodations, reliability/SEM, validity for the intended use, subgroup analyses, and prospective outcome evidence. Vendor technical studies are useful but should be labeled; research on an ancestor test is not automatic validation of a changed current product.

## Retrieval and completeness notes

All URLs above are direct primary publisher/government sources, publicly retrieved or found with official-source search on October 18, 2026. Explicit dates are given where the document itself supplies them. Undated product pages should be rechecked before purchasing or building an integration. No secondary review's “accuracy” rating, pricing, or estimated completion time was used as evidence. The My Next Move civil-engineer profile was retrieved directly; no numerical wage/outlook values are reproduced here. Missing disclosures above mean “not verified in this review,” not “does not exist.”
