# Subject reflection pilot — Approach 2 0.7.0

Status: ready for a small usability pilot. No student sessions have been conducted for this release. This is a usability exercise, not validation of a psychological instrument or prediction of student outcomes.

## What changed

Eight optional original Fieldguide course/project prompts provide a second exploration lens after the unchanged official 60-item O*NET profiler. Students choose up to two options per prompt, or mark unsure, skip or leave unanswered. At least two current directional answers are needed; each suggested pathway needs connections from at least two questions. Connected-question counts order starting points; the existing broad-interest order breaks ties. Broad ties defer subject ordering. Repeated explicit choices across two study areas can keep both visible. Fewer than four suggestions may be shown. Earlier broad answers stay saved for review without being silently narrowed. Students can pause and resume the subject lens without clearing answers. No pathway is hidden, no shortlist is changed, and neither earnings nor lifestyle goals affect order. The About page discloses every editorial mapping.

Pathway details and comparison now connect actual selections to subjects, a small stretch to try, education routes, existing earning evidence and the student's lifestyle goal. These are study associations, not ability judgments or admissions guidance.

## Suggested small pilot

Invite 5–8 students with varied levels of certainty about college subjects. A counselor or facilitator should use the same neutral prompts. Do not recruit or message anyone automatically. Use fictional/sample inputs during demos, and keep identifiable student records out of the repository and shared foundation. Let students opt out of any question and take breaks.

1. Start from a fresh draft or the explicitly fictional Jordan journey. Ask the student to describe what they think the product helps them decide.
2. Complete or inspect lifestyle choices and the official interest profiler. Record where students hesitate, without steering their answers.
3. Let the student decide whether to try the subject reflection. Ask them to think aloud. Observe whether they understand the two-choice limit, unsure, skip and pause controls.
4. Ask them to explain why one starting point appeared. Can they distinguish the original O*NET answers from Fieldguide's own prompts? Can they find another pathway outside the suggestions? For mixed interests, can they find both areas and explain why each appeared?
5. Compare two or three pathways. Ask which first-year subject, stretch or small experiment they would investigate next. Can they name a route difference without naming a profession?
6. Ask what the earning span means and whether it guarantees that a graduate will earn that amount. Check whether they understand that the selected occupation examples are not a graduate earnings distribution, and that college costs still need a separate program comparison.
7. Use “Compare broad interests only,” then resume the subject lens. Confirm answers are retained. If reviewing an older draft, check whether the student understands why an earlier broad choice needs review. Edit or clear only the subject reflection. Check whether the student expects their lifestyle goal, interest answers and shortlist to remain.

## Minimal observation sheet

Use anonymous session codes. Record completion/opt-out, confusing wording, ability to explain a suggestion, ability to find another pathway, interpretation of earnings, and one next action. Collect an optional “I feel able to choose a next exploration step” rating before and after; treat it as self-report only. Do not infer causal impact or predictive validity from a small convenience sample.

## Decision after the pilot

- Fix any repeated confusion about profession choice, ability claims, earning guarantees, or separate credential requirements before expansion.
- Revise prompts that students repeatedly cannot interpret or that steer too many unrelated areas together; inspect mappings with a counselor.
- Retain unsure/skipped answers as unknown. Do not improve apparent completion by supplying answers.
- Decide whether the extra questions help enough to keep as an optional step. Preserve a direct route to pathways.
- Once students can explain a pathway and its tradeoffs, scope a separate next phase for actual college programs, verified degree requirements, transfer time and net cost. This release does not supply those records.
